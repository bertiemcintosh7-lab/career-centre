Features

Track it, rank it,
walk in prepared

Three things it does, in the order you will need them.

Every application in one table

What you have sent, what stage it reached, what it came back as, and the single next thing you owe it. The daily brief tells you what is due before it is late.

  • Deadlines from the organisation's own page, with the date it was checked
  • Anything unpublished carries no date rather than a guess
  • Export the lot to CSV whenever you want it elsewhere

Ranked around what you actually want

Answer seven honest questions and the list reorders itself. Mark something useful or set it aside and it learns from that too — then prints the whole calculation so you can disagree with it.

  • Fit scored against your own answers, with the reason on the card
  • It never moves a dated card — a missed deadline costs more than a bad guess
  • Strengths, gaps and next steps built from your schooling and course

The questions they will actually ask

Per-field interview banks, the tests each sector sets, and reading that is worth the hour. Written for the field you picked, not a generic list wearing different labels.

  • What each sector screens on, and how the sift really works
  • Practice prompts with the answer structure that fits them
  • Employer routes: schemes, apprenticeships, direct applications
Why it is different

No account, no server, no catch

It is free because there is nothing to run. The page is the product.

Works offline

Once the page has loaded it needs nothing. On a train, in a library basement, on airplane mode the week before a deadline — it still opens.

Nothing to leak

Your answers stay in your own browser. There is no database holding them, which is a stronger guarantee than any promise about how carefully one would be guarded.

Sourced, or silent

Every date says where it came from and when it was checked. Where an employer has published nothing, the card says so instead of inventing a plausible deadline.

Privacy

It cannot leak what it never receives

Not a policy you have to take on trust — a consequence of how it is built.

No server behind it

Once the page has loaded it makes no further network requests. There is no backend to store your answers, and no log of what you typed.

Locked down by policy

A content security policy blocks the page from contacting anything at all — no scripts, no fonts, no images from anywhere but itself. It could not phone home if it tried.

The honest trade

Because nothing is stored anywhere else, clearing your browser data deletes it. So there is a back-up button, and it matters. Nobody can reset a passcode for you either.

In the calendar now

Real dates, read off the source

Four of the twenty in the app, each checked against the organisation's own page on 12 August 2026.
